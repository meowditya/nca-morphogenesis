How it works

Every cell carries 16 numbers: red, green, blue, alpha and 12 hidden channels it can use as chemical signals. A cell with alpha above 0.1 is mature, cells next to a mature one are growing, and everything else is dead and stays zero.

Each step a cell senses its 3×3 neighbourhood through fixed identity and Sobel filters (48 values). It feeds them through a small two-layer network shared by every cell to get an update, which a living cell applies on a coin flip. Nothing is global: no cell knows where it is.

Training starts from a single seed cell and unrolls 64-96 steps. The loss compares the RGBA channels with the target. A pool of past results is reused so the pattern learns to stay stable, and random discs are erased from the best samples so it learns to regrow.

Walls are cells forced to zero every step. The network never saw them during training, so how it routes around them is pure generalisation.
